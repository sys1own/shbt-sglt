Synthetic Gravitational Lensing Telescope (SHBT-SGLT): Architectural Specification, Physics Transfer Matrix, and Hardware-in-the-Loop Implementation BlueprintThe development of space-based synthetic gravitational lensing systems requires synthesizing quantum metric engineering, ultra-low-noise heterodyne laser metrology, multi-tier guidance, navigation, and control (GNC), active lattice-assisted nuclear reaction (LANR) power infrastructure, and high-speed bare-metal hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) microkernel execution. The shbt-sglt software platform establishes the primary digital-twin and HIL co-simulation suite for the Synthetic Gravitational Lensing Telescope (SGLT) mission architecture. This report provides the architectural specification for building shbt-sglt, detailing the software directory topology, cross-repository code transfer matrix, analytical flight dynamics and power derating formulations, and the complete master HIL system verification framework.1. The sglt-core-metric crate handles the 512-bit Arnowitt–Deser–Misner (ADM) metric calculations using arbitrary-precision mathematical bindings (rug/MPFR). It evaluates the spacetime interference tensor $I_{\mu\nu}$, non-paraxial eikonal wave pre-distortion, and holographic scale reconciliation parameters ($\chi_{\text{SHBT}}$) that anchor boundary states to effective spatial metrics.Flight dynamics, laser metrology, and multi-tier attitude control are encapsulated within sglt-flight-gnc. This sub-crate simulates the relative trajectories of two spacecraft operating at the Sun–Earth $L_2$ point ($SE\text{-}L_2$), incorporating solar radiation pressure, tidal gravity gradients, and outgassing perturbations. It drives dual-wavelength heterodyne laser interferometry models, Differential Wavefront Sensing (DWS) routines, and a 2-tier control system split between coarse electrospray thrusters and fine electro-optic phase shifters.Optoelectronic array behavior and acoustic wave propagation are processed by sglt-transducer-fea. This sub-crate maps digital control signals to an $8\times 8$ Indium Phosphide / Indium Gallium Arsenide (InP/InGaAs) Heterojunction Bipolar Transistor (HBT) phase-shifter DAC. It performs transient Finite Element Analysis (FEA) across single-crystal sapphire waveguides and quarter-wave nanoporous silica aerogel tamping layers while accounting for thermal dissipation governed by Debye $T^3$ heat capacity relationships.Power generation and fault recovery logic are implemented in sglt-lanr-power. It tracks the thermal and electrical performance of an 1,800-module LANR cold fusion power plant delivering $913.18\text{ kW}$ gross power against a continuous system demand of $906.00\text{ kW}$. When reactor modules experience quenches or operational degradation, the crate executes an $N-k$ fault-tolerant derating protocol that converts lost electrical output into bit decrements, systematically adjusting the synthetic boundary seed mass ($M_{\text{seed}}$) and expanding the optical focal length ($f$).The sglt-hil-microkernel sub-crate connects high-level Rust simulation pipelines to bare-metal hardware systems. It links directly to the freestanding C11 runtime (shbt-os), managing AVX-512 SIMD current shunt monitoring loops, SECDED Hamming(72,64) ECC validation, automated sub-120 ns post-quench recovery drivers, and high-speed POSIX zero-copy shared memory transports (shm_open/mmap).Image formation and optical verification are managed by sglt-astro-reconstruction. This crate processes simulated focal plane photon intensity maps using a regularized modified Richardson–Lucy deconvolution algorithm. It evaluates synthetic Point Spread Functions (PSF) and optical Strehl ratios ($S \ge 0.999999980$) to confirm that lensed wavefronts achieve an angular resolution of $0.0629''$ with an amplitude magnification factor of $\mu_{\text{amp}} = 1.2566 \times 10^7$.Outside the Rust workspace, kernel/ provides the freestanding C11 source code, assembly startup files, custom linker script (linker.ld), and memory-mapped register maps (shbt_hardware.h) governing physical address space 0x70000000 (SHBT-MMIO-1). The eda/ directory houses automated exporter scripts for exporting semiconductor layout GDSII masks and RF interposerTouchstone parameters. The Python layer (python/shbt_sglt/) bridges Rust data structures to high-level analysis pipelines via PyO3 dynamic bindings, powering a Streamlit telemetry HUD and unified Command-Line Interface (CLI).2. Flight Dynamics, GNC & $N-k$ Power Derating SpecificationsFormation Flight Dynamics at Sun–Earth $L_2$The SGLT space segment consists of two spacecraft flying in a synchronized formation within a synodic frame centered at the Sun–Earth $L_2$ libration point: the Lens Craft ($M_1 = 5,100\text{ kg}$ wet mass) and the Sensor Craft ($M_2 = 1,460\text{ kg}$ wet mass). The vector $\mathbf{\rho} = [x, y, z]^T$ represents the relative displacement of the Sensor Craft relative to the Lens Craft. Relative orbital motion incorporates differential solar radiation pressure ($\Delta \mathbf{a}_{\text{SRP}}$), the linearized Sun–Earth tidal gravity tensor ($C_{\text{tidal}}$), outgassing decay forces ($\mathbf{a}_{\text{outgas}}$), and control thrust inputs ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{control}}$):$$\ddot{\mathbf{\rho}} + 2\mathbf{\Omega} \times \dot{\mathbf{\rho}} + \mathbf{\Omega} \times (\mathbf{\Omega} \times \mathbf{\rho}) = C_{\text{tidal}} \mathbf{\rho} + \Delta \mathbf{a}_{\text{SRP}} + \mathbf{a}_{\text{outgas}} + \frac{\mathbf{F}_{\text{control}}}{M_2}$$The vector $\mathbf{\Omega} = [0, 0, \omega_0]^T$ represents the angular velocity of the Sun–Earth rotating reference frame, where $\omega_0 = 1.990986 \times 10^{-7}\text{ rad/s}$. The linearized tidal gravity matrix $C_{\text{tidal}}$ is derived from the gravitational potential gradient at the $L_2$ point:$$C_{\text{tidal}} = \text{diag}\left(2\sigma + 1, \, 1 - \sigma, \, -\sigma\right) \cdot \omega_0^2$$where $\sigma \approx 3.0428$ represents the $L_2$ libration parameter. Differential solar radiation pressure arises from area-to-mass ratio discrepancies and optical surface reflectivity mismatches between the two spacecraft:$$\Delta \mathbf{a}_{\text{SRP}} = P_{\text{solar}} \left( \frac{A_1 C_{R,1}}{M_1} - \frac{A_2 C_{R,2}}{M_2} \right) \hat{\mathbf{S}} \approx 3.42 \times 10^{-8} \hat{\mathbf{S}} \quad \left[\text{m/s}^2\right]$$where $P_{\text{solar}} = 4.56 \times 10^{-6}\text{ N/m}^2$ is the nominal solar radiation pressure at 1 AU, $A_1 = 28.5\text{ m}^2$ and $A_2 = 6.2\text{ m}^2$ are the effective cross-sectional areas, $C_{R,1} = 1.25$ and $C_{R,2} = 1.10$ are surface reflectivity coefficients, and $\hat{\mathbf{S}}$ is the Sun-pointing unit vector. Residual outgassing follows an exponential decay profile:$$\mathbf{a}_{\text{outgas}}(t) = a_0 \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{t}{\tau_{\text{degas}}}\right) \hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\text{out}} \approx 5.0 \times 10^{-9} \exp\left(-\frac{t}{180 \text{ days}}\right) \quad \left[\text{m/s}^2\right]$$Thermal expansion within the Carbon-Fiber-Reinforced Silicon Carbide (C/SiC) optical metering structures induces physical baseline drift at a rate of $0.45\text{ nm/K}$.To counter these environmental disturbances, the 2-tier hybrid GNC controller splits authority across two frequency bands:Coarse Low-Frequency Control ($f < 0.1\text{ Hz}$): Managed by ionic liquid electrospray thrusters ($\text{EMI-BF}_4$ propellant) producing continuous thrust between $0.1\ \mu\text{N}$ and $150.0\ \mu\text{N}$ at a specific impulse $I_{\text{sp}} = 3,200\text{ s}$. This loop compensates for orbital drift, solar radiation pressure, and station-keeping requirements.Fine High-Frequency Control ($0.1\text{ Hz} \le f \le 100\text{ kHz}$): Managed by an integrated $8\times 8$ InP electro-optic phase-shifter array operating between $3.8\text{ V}$ and $7.4\text{ V}$. This optoelectronic loop corrects micro-vibrations, thermal expansion drift, and transient optical phase jitter, maintaining absolute baseline displacement errors within $\Vert{}\delta \mathbf{r}\Vert{}_{3\sigma} \le 1.00\text{ nm}$ and DWS attitude tracking within $\sigma_\theta \le 15\text{ nrad}$.$N-k$ Fault-Tolerant Seed-Mass Derating ProtocolThe synthetic gravitational lensing metric is sustained by an 1,800-module LANR cold fusion reactor cluster. The array provides a nominal gross electrical output of $913.18\text{ kW}$ ($+507.32\text{ W}_{\text{net}}$ per module at a thermoelectric generator efficiency of $33.80\%$) against a continuous baseline entropy debt demand of $906.00\text{ kW}$. This leaves an operational net reserve margin of $+7.18\text{ kW}$, equivalent to $N+14$ active reserve modules.When $k$ reactor modules fail, the loss of net power capacity is expressed as:$$\Delta P_{\text{net}}(k) = k \cdot P_{\text{module}} = k \cdot 507.32 \quad [\text{W}]$$To prevent register underflow and maintain numerical stability across hardware-in-the-loop interfaces, power losses are converted into discrete 512-bit register bit decrements using the conversion factor $P_{\text{bit}} \approx 8.9506 \times 10^{-4}\text{ W/bit}$:$$\Delta N(k) = \left\lfloor \frac{\Delta P_{\text{net}}(k)}{P_{\text{bit}}} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{k \cdot 507.32}{8.9506 \times 10^{-4}} \right\rfloor \quad [\text{bits}]$$The mass-coupling engine adjusts the effective boundary mass parameter $M_{\text{seed}}$ using the scaling parameter $\alpha_{\text{seed}} = 1.3258 \times 10^{-51} M_\odot/\text{bit}$:$$M_{\text{seed}}(N-k) = M_{\text{seed, nominal}} - \alpha_{\text{seed}} \cdot \Delta N(k)$$As module failures accumulate, $M_{\text{seed}}$ decreases continuously from its nominal value of $10^{-6} M_\odot$ ($906.00\text{ kW}$ net entropy balance) down to $10^{-7} M_\odot$ ($90.60\text{ kW}$ degraded power limit). To maintain optical phase alignment across the lensed wavefront, the spacecraft control system adjusts the inter-craft focal baseline distance ($f$) inversely with $M_{\text{seed}}$:$$f(M_{\text{seed}}) = f_0 \cdot \left( \frac{M_{\text{seed, nominal}}}{M_{\text{seed}}(N-k)} \right)$$Under nominal operating conditions ($k=0$), $M_{\text{seed}} = 10^{-6} M_\odot$, yielding an inter-craft focal baseline $f_0 = 169.30\text{ m}$. At the maximum survivable fault limit of $k = 1,607$ failed modules ($10\%$ residual power capacity, $M_{\text{seed}} = 10^{-7} M_\odot$), the GNC system increases the inter-craft baseline to $f_{\text{max}} = 1,692.99\text{ m}$.Sensor Craft 5th-Order Minimum-Jerk Repositioning TrajectoryWhen repositioning the Sensor Craft along the line-of-sight axis to adapt to a changed focal distance $f(M_{\text{seed}})$, the GNC system executes a 5th-order minimum-jerk trajectory profile. This smooth acceleration profile prevents structural resonance in the optical metering assembly. Using normalized dimensionless time $\tau = t / T_{\text{slew}} \in [0, 1]$, the dimensionless displacement $s(\tau)$ is defined as:$$s(\tau) = 10\tau^3 - 15\tau^4 + 6\tau^5$$The normalized kinematic derivatives for velocity $\dot{s}(\tau)$, acceleration $\ddot{s}(\tau)$, and jerk $\dddot{s}(\tau)$ are derived as follows:$$\dot{s}(\tau) = \frac{d s}{d \tau} = 30\tau^2 - 60\tau^3 + 30\tau^4 = 30\tau^2 (1 - \tau)^2$$$$\ddot{s}(\tau) = \frac{d^2 s}{d \tau^2} = 60\tau - 180\tau^2 + 120\tau^3 = 60\tau (1 - \tau)(1 - 2\tau)$$$$\dddot{s}(\tau) = \frac{d^3 s}{d \tau^3} = 60 - 360\tau + 360\tau^2 = 60 (1 - 6\tau + 6\tau^2)$$Analyzing these kinematic profiles yields exact maximum values:Maximum Velocity: Evaluating $d\dot{s}/d\tau = \ddot{s}(\tau) = 0$ yields a peak velocity at $\tau = 0.5$. Substituting $\tau = 0.5$ gives $\max(\dot{s}) = 30(0.5)^2(1 - 0.5)^2 = 1.8750$.Maximum Acceleration: Evaluating $d\ddot{s}/d\tau = \dddot{s}(\tau) = 0$ yields inflection roots at $\tau = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{3}}{6} \approx 0.2113 \text{ and } 0.7887$. Substituting these roots gives peak dimensionless acceleration $\max(\vert{}\ddot{s}\vert{}) = \frac{10}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 5.7735$, bounded within numerical tolerances at $5.7733$.10-Year Propellant Consumption BudgetStation-keeping, formation maintenance, and focal repositioning maneuvers are performed by ionic liquid electrospray thrusters using $\text{EMI-BF}_4$ propellant with a specific impulse $I_{\text{sp}} = 3,200\text{ s}$ ($v_e = I_{\text{sp}} g_0 = 31,381.28\text{ m/s}$).Applying Tsiolkovsky’s rocket equation, $m_{\text{prop}} = M_0 \left( 1 - e^{-\Delta V / v_e} \right)$:Lens Craft ($M_1 = 5,100\text{ kg}$): Allocated 10-year total velocity increment $\Delta V_1 = 52.370\text{ m/s}$.$$m_{\text{prop, 1}} = 5100 \cdot \left( 1 - \exp\left( -\frac{52.370}{31381.28} \right) \right) = 7.0900 \quad [\text{kg}]$$Sensor Craft ($M_2 = 1,460\text{ kg}$): Allocated 10-year total velocity increment $\Delta V_2 = 62.370\text{ m/s}$.$$m_{\text{prop, 2}} = 1460 \cdot \left( 1 - \exp\left( -\frac{62.370}{31381.28} \right) \right) = 3.3800 \quad [\text{kg}]$$With onboard tank capacities of $450\text{ kg}$ per spacecraft, total 10-year propellant consumption consumes less than $2\%$ of reserves, leaving $>98\%$ fuel margin at End-of-Life (EOL).4.
