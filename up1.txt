Multi-Spectral Relativistic Optics & FPA Sensor PipelineWideband Multi-Spectral Wave OpticsThe solar gravitational lens utilizes the spherically symmetric static spacetime described by the first post-Newtonian ($1\text{PN}$) metric. For electromagnetic radiation propagating in the wavelength band $\lambda \in [200\text{ nm}, 5.0\ \mu\text{m}]$, light paths bend around the solar mass $M_\odot$ with an impact parameter $b$. The gravitational deflection angle $\theta$ is derived from Einstein's field equations:$$\theta(b) = \frac{4GM_\odot}{c^2 b} = \frac{2r_g}{b}$$where $r_g = \frac{2GM_\odot}{c^2} \approx 2.95\text{ km}$ represents the Schwarzschild radius of the Sun. Because rays with larger impact parameters bend less, the focal line begins at $z_0 = \frac{R_\odot^2}{2r_g} \approx 547.8\text{ AU}$ and extends outwards continuously along the optical axis.Light rays propagating from distant sources pass the solar perimeter at an impact parameter $b$, bending toward the optical axis where they converge beyond $547.8\text{ AU}$ to form a continuous focal line. To account for multi-spectral dispersion caused by solar corona plasma densities and frequency-dependent boundary conditions along the optical axis, the effective focal length $f(\lambda)$ at receiver plane distance $z$ is formulated as:$$f(\lambda) = \frac{r_0^2 c^2}{4 G M_{\text{seed}}(\lambda)}$$where $M_{\text{seed}}(\lambda)$ incorporates the effective mass-energy distribution and plasma refractive modifications $\delta n(\lambda, r) = -\frac{e^2 N_e(r)}{2 \epsilon_0 m_e \omega^2}$, with $\omega = \frac{2\pi c}{\lambda}$.Relativistic 3D Wave-Optics RaytracingThe spatial field distribution $E(\mathbf{r}, \lambda)$ near the focal line is governed by Maxwell's equations in curved spacetime. Solving the Helmholtz equation via the eikonal phase shift approximation yields the complex amplitude at image coordinates $(\rho, \phi, z)$:$$E(\rho, \phi, z; \lambda) = E_0 \, e^{i k z} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{2\pi^2 r_g}{\lambda}} \, J_0\left( \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \sqrt{\frac{2r_g}{z}} \, \rho \right) \exp\left( i \left[ \frac{k \rho^2}{2z} + \frac{r_g}{\lambda} \ln(2 k z) \right] \right)$$where $J_0$ is the zeroth-order Bessel function of the first kind, $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$, and $\rho = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ is the radial displacement from the optical center. The light amplification factor $\mu_0(\lambda)$ along the central optical axis ($\rho = 0$) evaluates to:$$\mu_0(\lambda) = \frac{4\pi^2 r_g}{\lambda}$$For an extended exoplanetary disk source of radius $R_s$ at distance $d_s$, the total irradiance $I_{\text{optical}}(x,y)$ on the image plane is the convolution of the source intensity distribution $B(x', y'; \lambda)$ with the SGL Point Spread Function (PSF) $P(\rho; \lambda) = \vert{}E(\rho, \lambda)\vert{}^2$:$$I_{\text{optical}}(x,y) = \int_{\Omega_s} B(x', y'; \lambda) \cdot P\left( \sqrt{(x - x')^2 + (y - y')^2}; \lambda \right) \, dx' \, dy'$$Focal Plane Array (FPA) Sensor Noise ModelThe target focal plane array integrates custom CMOS/EMCCD hybrid detectors optimized across three optical bands: Ultraviolet-Visible (200–400 nm), Visible-NIR (400–1100 nm), and Short-Wave/Mid-Wave IR (1.1–5.0 $\mu\text{m}$). Ephemeris-Driven 4-Body CR3BP & Radiation EnvironmentRotating Frame Equations of MotionThe astrodynamical solver models spacecraft trajectory evolution within the four-body Circular Restricted Three-Body Problem (CR3BP) encompassing the Sun ($m_1$), Earth-Moon system ($m_2$), and Jupiter ($m_3$), augmented by Solar Radiation Pressure (SRP) acceleration $\mathbf{a}_{\text{SRP}}$.In the rotating frame centered at the Sun-Earth barycenter, the normalized equations of motion are given by:$$\ddot{x} - 2\dot{y} = \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial x} + a_{\text{SRP}, x} + a_{\text{Jup}, x}$$$$\ddot{y} + 2\dot{x} = \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial y} + a_{\text{SRP}, y} + a_{\text{Jup}, y}$$$$\ddot{z} = \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial z} + a_{\text{SRP}, z} + a_{\text{Jup}, z}$$The effective scalar potential $\Omega(x,y,z)$ is defined as:$$\Omega(x,y,z) = \frac{1}{2}\left(x^2 + y^2\right) + \frac{1 - \mu}{r_1} + \frac{\mu}{r_2} + \frac{1}{2}\mu(1-\mu)$$where $\mu = \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$ is the mass ratio, $r_1 = \sqrt{(x+\mu)^2 + y^2 + z^2}$, and $r_2 = \sqrt{(x-1+\mu)^2 + y^2 + z^2}$. The Solar Radiation Pressure vector is modeled via the optical force coefficient $C_R$ and area-to-mass ratio $\frac{A}{m}$:$$\mathbf{a}_{\text{SRP}} = \frac{S_0 \, C_R \, A}{c \, m \cdot r_1^2} \left( \frac{r_{1,0}}{r_1} \right)^2 \hat{\mathbf{r}}_1$$where $S_0 = 1361\text{ W/m}^2$ is the solar constant at $r_{1,0} = 1\text{ AU}$. To ensure numerical stability over 100-year integration arcs, the state vector $\mathbf{X} = [x, y, z, \dot{x}, \dot{y}, \dot{z}]^T$ monitors conservation of the Jacobi Energy Constant $C_J$:$$C_J = 2\Omega(x,y,z) - \left( \dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2 + \dot{z}^2 \right)$$The adaptive step control enforces absolute error tolerance $\epsilon_{\text{abs}} \le 10^{-14}$ and relative error tolerance $\epsilon_{\text{rel}} \le 10^{-12}$, maintaining conservation of $C_J$ such that:$$\vert{}\Delta C_J(t)\vert{} = \vert{}C_J(t) - C_J(0)\vert{} \le 10^{-12}$$Radiation Environment & Semiconductor DegradationBeyond $500\text{ AU}$, spacecraft microelectronics encounter Galactic Cosmic Ray (GCR) primary protons/heavy ions and Solar Proton Events (SPE).
